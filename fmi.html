Financial Market Infrastructure

Post-trade is the weakest link in Nigeria's capital market — and the least understood. We bring the operating discipline of the world's leading clearing houses to the regulators and institutions that clear, settle, report and safeguard the nation's capital.

The Thesis

Markets are made or broken after the trade

Financial market infrastructure — FMI — is the set of institutions through which money and securities move: exchanges, central counterparties, central securities depositories, trade repositories and settlement systems. Nigeria has spent two decades modernizing the front office — trading, listings, market data — and under-invested in the post-trade layer beneath it: clearing, settlement, collateral and default management. Globally, the majority of capital-markets operational losses originate in exactly that layer.

The reform cycle has now arrived. On 1 June 2026, Nigeria compressed its settlement cycle to T+1 — joining a small group of global markets — placing real operational demands on every custodian, broker and depository. A new generation of clearing institutions is taking shape, yet the segment remains under-capitalized and under-served by advisors who have actually run this machinery.

Three pieces of infrastructure are already implied by Nigeria's own rules and do not yet exist: a trade repository for over-the-counter derivatives, which the Derivatives Trading Rules require; a repo market deep enough to carry the Nigerian Overnight Financing Rate (NOFR), the new benchmark administered by the Central Bank; and a reporting layer for naira–stablecoin conversions that the draft digital-asset rules presume. Completing them is a matter of design and delivery, not new policy.

Our principal spent two decades on Wall Street, including years at a leading global clearing house, working on the US transition to T+1 and on derivatives central clearing. That operating fluency in post-trade is precisely what the moment demands, and precisely what is scarce in the Nigerian market. That is the gap we close.

The Stack

Where we focus across the infrastructure layer

From the moment a trade is struck to the moment value finally settles, every step depends on dedicated infrastructure. We work across the full stack — for the regulators who set the rules and the operators who run the machinery.

Central Counterparties & Clearing

The institutions that stand between buyer and seller to guarantee trades and contain systemic risk — where our principal's experience runs deepest, and an emerging segment in Nigeria.

Settlement & Depositories

The custody, book-entry settlement and registrar backbone — the registry of ownership on which the entire market relies, now operating on a T+1 cycle.

Default Management & Risk

Default waterfalls, margin methodology and the stress-testing that keeps a clearing house solvent when a member fails — the work few in-country have done at depth.

Trade Repositories & Reporting

The post-trade reporting layer every G20 market built after 2008. Nigeria's rules require it and its licence category exists; the institution does not. Our first venture.

Repo Markets & Collateral

Repo guidelines, standard documentation, eligible-collateral schedules and tri-party collateral management — the market a benchmark like NOFR needs beneath it.

T+1 & Post-Trade Operations

Affirmation and confirmation discipline, fails management and straight-through processing — the operational reality of a compressed settlement cycle, in force for Nigerian equities since 1 June 2026.
